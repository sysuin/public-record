Which Indian airline is actually the most reliable?

We read 104 monthly reports from the Directorate General of Civil Aviation, January 2018 to September 2026. The airline that leaves on time most often, the one that cancels least and the one with the fewest complaints have not been the same airline in a single month.

Scope: scheduled domestic flights, as reported by the airlines to DGCA · On-time figures cover departures from the listed airports only

The answer everyone assumes

Every month DGCA publishes an on-time table, the winner advertises it, and the press repeats it. The working answer is that IndiGo is India's most reliable airline. It has topped the table in 53 of 104 months since January 2018, and in every month of 2026.

The source

DGCA's monthly domestic traffic report is free to download at dgca.gov.in (Data & Reports → Aviation Data & Statistics → Air Transport → Domestic). For each airline it gives passengers carried, the share of flights that left on time, the share cancelled, and complaints per 10,000 passengers. We read every report from January 2018 to September 2026; April 2020 was never published. To see what the on-time figure covers, we also used DGCA's approved winter 2026 schedules and the Airports Authority of India's airport traffic tables.

Every report says it is "prepared based on information received from scheduled domestic airlines". These are the airlines' own figures, and DGCA publishes no flight counts behind any percentage.

What the obvious number says

In January to September 2026, at the airports DGCA measures, IndiGo left on time 86.0% of the time, Akasa Air 80.8% and the Air India Group 79.5%. That lead is bigger than the normal month-to-month swing: reshuffling the nine months thousands of times keeps IndiGo's lead over Akasa between 2.3 and 8.1 points, with IndiGo on top every time. In 2025 the lead was smaller and just as steady, 3.2 points.

That is a recent pattern. Over the whole record the title changed hands 35 times.

In 2018–19 Go First beat IndiGo by 0.95 points on average; in 2022–24 IndiGo beat Vistara by 0.92. Both gaps are smaller than one airline's normal swing from month to month, so in those years the winner was close to a coin toss.

The turn: what the on-time number leaves out

It counts departures from a few airports. Until December 2024 only Bengaluru, Delhi, Hyderabad and Mumbai counted. Chennai and Kolkata were added in 2025, and Ahmedabad, Kochi, Guwahati and Lucknow in 2026. The original four handle about 35% of India's domestic aircraft movements.

On today's schedule the original four airports carry 38% of IndiGo's departures and 56% of the Air India Group's. Even the ten airports measured in 2026 cover 58% of IndiGo's departures and 60% of all scheduled domestic departures.

The airport moves the number almost as much as the airline. In 2026, flights from Chennai left on time 93.1% of the time and flights from Mumbai 73.9%.

If the Air India Group flew IndiGo's mix of airports, its 2026 score would rise from 79.5% to about 81.1%, and IndiGo's lead would shrink from 6.5 to 4.9 points. The lead survives, but roughly a quarter of it comes from where IndiGo flies.

Punctual is not the same as reliable. In none of 102 months was one airline outright best on punctuality, cancellations and complaints together. The most punctual airline also cancelled least in only 23% of months.

Akasa cancelled 0.13% of flights, about 1 in 770, against 0.41% for IndiGo (1 in 240) and 0.74% for the Air India Group (1 in 140). On complaints, Akasa (2.52) and IndiGo (2.63) cannot be separated.

The worst month in eight years

In December 2025, IndiGo cancelled 9.65% of its flights, 15.6 times its typical month since 2022 (0.62%) and nearly double its previous worst (5.0%, in the fog of January 2024). Airlines received 29,212 complaints that month, more than in the whole of 2024. DGCA's report puts 63.9% of the month's cancellations down to operational reasons and 31.6% to weather.

That one month decides any single "most reliable" score. Combine the three measures with every reasonable weighting, and over October 2025 to September 2026 Akasa comes out on top under 88% of them. Remove December 2025 alone and IndiGo comes out on top under 71%.

Complaints are up, and baggage now leads

In a typical month, complaints per 10,000 passengers went from 0.57 in 2024 to 2.09 in 2026, a rise across every large airline. Baggage went from 19.8% of complaints to 43.1%. That is still about one complaint per 4,800 passengers, and the reports do not say whether people complained more or complaints were counted differently.

The corrected answer

There is a trade-off rather than one winner.

  • Leaves on time from a big airport: IndiGo, clearly, since 2025: 5.2 points ahead of Akasa in 2026. Against the Air India Group the lead is 6.5 points, or 4.9 on the same mix of airports.
  • Your flight actually operates: Akasa, clearly, in 2025 and 2026.
  • Fewest complaints: no clear winner in 2026.
  • One combined score: depends on the weights and on one month.

Two popular explanations did not survive. Airlines do not stay punctual by cancelling: months with more cancellations had worse on-time rates, not better. And bad months are not just fog. Fog season costs about 10 points of punctuality (70.7% in December–January against 80.3% otherwise), but the worst cancellation month on record was labelled mostly operational.

IndiGo is the most punctual large airline at the airports DGCA measures. Akasa is the least likely to cancel. No airline has led outright on all three measures in any month since 2018.

What this data cannot tell you

  • Anything independent of the airlines. Every figure is self-reported to DGCA.
  • Arrivals, or most airports. On-time means departing within 15 minutes, at the listed airports only. Pune, Goa, Jaipur, Navi Mumbai and over a hundred others never enter the table.
  • How many flights sit behind any percentage. No counts are published.
  • Like-for-like years. The airport list changed in 2025 and again in 2026.
  • A stable "Air India". The group absorbed Air India Express, AIX Connect (October 2024) and Vistara (November 2024).
  • Why complaints rose, or what caused any delay. Causes are the airlines' own labels, and knock-on delays (about two-thirds of the total) hide the original cause.
  • Safety. Nothing here measures it, and nothing here should be read as a safety ranking.

How we did it

Source. 104 DGCA monthly domestic traffic reports (PDF), January 2018 to September 2026, plus DGCA's winter 2026 approved schedules and AAI's 2026 domestic movement tables. All free to download, with no login.

Extraction. Every report was read by two independent PDF methods. A ten-month spot-check matched 265 of 265 chart values and 220 of 220 table values, and airline market shares sum to 97–100% in every month.

Comparisons. Airlines with at least 1% of passengers that month. Ranges come from reshuffling months in three-month blocks, so a lead is only called a lead when it survives that.

Checks. Every headline number was computed a second time by independent code.

Reproduce it. Scripts, derived data, the research file and the exclusion log: github.com/sysuin/public-record/tree/main/r03-india-airlines. Every number on this page is read from them at build time.
